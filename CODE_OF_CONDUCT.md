# Code of conduct

MonitorFlow is made by people who run servers, for people who run servers. Whether you open an issue, send a pull request, review code or answer a question, you should feel welcome here and be treated with respect.

This code applies to everyone who takes part: the maintainer, contributors, people asking for help and people helping them.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Many people here are new to Rust, to monitoring or to open source. A question that looks simple to you may have taken courage to ask.
- **Assume good intent.** Written text loses its tone. Ask what someone meant before deciding they meant harm.
- **Comment on the work, not the person.** "This writes the token to the log" helps. "Whoever wrote this has no idea" does not.
- **Accept a no.** The project has one maintainer and a roadmap. Not every idea or pull request will be accepted, and that is not a judgement of you.
- **Give credit.** Name the people whose work or ideas you build on.
- **Protect people's data.** Before you paste logs, screenshots or configuration, remove passwords, tokens, keys, certificates, addresses and customer names. Never post someone else's private data.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, slurs or demeaning remarks about anyone, including about their age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual orientation or any other personal trait.
- Harassment, in public or in private: unwanted contact that continues after being asked to stop, following someone across platforms, intimidation or threats.
- Sexual language or images, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Trolling, deliberately derailing a discussion, and spam. This includes opening issues or pull requests in bulk with content you have not read and checked yourself, whether you wrote it or a tool did.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as their address, email or private messages, without their permission.
- Pressuring the maintainer or contributors with demands for fixes, features or deadlines. MonitorFlow is free and maintained on a best-effort basis.
- Encouraging or helping anyone to do any of the above.

## Where it applies

This code applies in every MonitorFlow space: the GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, commit messages) and any chat or forum the project opens later. It also applies when you speak for MonitorFlow elsewhere, for example at an event or on social media.

Behaviour outside these spaces can be taken into account when it puts people in the project at risk.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour makes you uncomfortable, or you see something that breaks this code, tell the maintainer privately at **conduct@monitorflow.fr**. Please do not answer it in public: that often makes things worse.

Say what happened, where (a link if you have one), when, and who was involved. Screenshots help, since comments can be edited or deleted.

Every report is read. Your name and the details stay private and are shared only with your permission. Nobody will be punished for a report made in good faith.

If the report is about the maintainer, or you would rather not contact the project, report it to GitHub: use the **...** menu on any comment, or **Block or report** on a user's profile.

Security vulnerabilities do not belong here: see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## What happens next

The maintainer reviews the report, may ask you or others questions, and decides on a response that fits what happened. From the lightest to the most serious:

1. **A correction.** A private note explaining what was wrong, sometimes with a request to edit or remove a comment.
2. **A warning.** A clear statement of what must stop, and what happens if it continues.
3. **A temporary ban.** No interaction with the project for a set period.
4. **A permanent ban.** No interaction with the project at all.

Serious cases, such as threats or sustained harassment, can go straight to a ban. The maintainer can also edit, hide, lock or remove comments, issues, pull requests, commits and other contributions that break this code.

MonitorFlow is maintained by one person, so there is no set deadline for a reply. Reports about someone's safety come first.

## Changes and credits

This code can change. Changes are proposed in a pull request, like any other change to the project.

It draws on the ideas of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/) and other open source codes of conduct, rewritten for MonitorFlow.
